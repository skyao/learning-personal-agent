The discussions are fluid, per Fortune, and it is unclear if or how soon an agreement might be struck. Both companies declined to comment, per the same report. Amazon access is still blocked today.

## What Fortune reports, and what it does not

Fortune bases its report on people familiar with the matter. The companies have held talks on multiple occasions, per those people, as Fortune reports it.

Two major factors in the negotiations are how the companies would handle search results and advertising, per Fortune. Fortune also says numerous issues remain, ranging from Amazon's own AI shopping ambitions to how exactly Muse would connect to Amazon's store.

Any deal would likely require the sign off of the chief executives of both companies, per Fortune. That sentence sets the level of the talks and the distance left. A chief executive sign off is not a launch step. It is a gate that has not been reported as passed.

No Amazon statement and no Meta statement appear in the report. Fortune says Meta and Amazon declined to comment. ## Why Amazon blocked Muse in September

Amazon blocked the Muse app from accessing its store in September 2026, saying Muse access would violate its user agreements, per Fortune. Muse was released in the United States that month.

That blockade has shaped shopping in Muse since September. The BBC trial published November 24, 2026, covered in the UK launch post on this site, reported an agent spending an hour saying an Amazon page was still loading. The Walmart post published November 24, 2026 on this site reported the same contrast from The Verge: Walmart opened a gateway, while Amazon and eBay blocked agents. Fortune is the first report on this site's record that Amazon and Meta are talking about removing the Amazon block. Talking is the whole of it so far.

## The first sticking point: search results

If Muse connects to Amazon's store, a shopper will ask Muse for a product and Muse will surface results. Which products come first, and why, is a negotiation issue, per Fortune.

Fortune reports Amazon is worried about the quality of the product search results Muse would surface. That worry is printed as Amazon's, in Fortune's reporting. No test of Muse search results inside Amazon exists in the November 25 record, because there is no connection to test.

A reader should hold the question in that form. The issue is not whether Muse search is good or bad in the abstract. The issue Fortune reports is whether Amazon will accept the results Muse surfaces inside Amazon's own store, where Amazon controls ranking today. No ranking rule, no sponsored placement rule, and no quality standard that both companies have accepted was published on November 25.

## The second sticking point: advertising

Fortune reports that Muse could in theory bypass ads on Amazon's website. Amazon is worried about losing out on advertising revenue, per Fortune.

The size of that revenue is in Fortune, attributed there: advertising is a $68.6 billion business for Amazon, representing nearly 10 percent of Amazon's total yearly revenue. I am printing that figure as Fortune reports it. It is not a figure from an Amazon filing read for this post.

That figure explains the shape of the negotiation without needing a theory about either company. If an agent shops without seeing the ads a human shopper sees on Amazon's site, the ad business loses the visit. If the agent does see ads, or pays for placement, or shows sponsored results first, the shopper needs to know that rule before trusting the list. No rule of that kind was published on November 25.

Fortune also reports a second Amazon concern next to ads and search: executives are concerned that shopping agents like Muse will weaken the relationship Amazon has with consumers. That concern is also printed as reported by Fortune, from people familiar with the matter. No Amazon executive is quoted stating it in the article.

## The scale figures Fortune cites: 10 million setups, a $190 billion to $385 billion forecast

Three context figures appear in Fortune. Each belongs to its source, not to this site.

More than 10 million people in the United States and Canada had set up Muse as of October, per Fortune, which calls it Meta's biggest consumer AI hit to date. Zuckerberg has said shopping is Muse's top use case, per Fortune. Meta eventually plans to profit by taking a small fee from transactions, per Fortune, in wording that matches the transaction fee point CNBC reported on November 24, 2026 from Meta's last earnings call coverage.

A 2025 Morgan Stanley report, as Fortune cites it, puts agentic shopping at $190 billion to $385 billion in United States e-commerce sales by 2030, or 10 percent to 20 percent of the total market. That is a 2025 forecast for a whole category. It is not a sales figure for Muse, and it is not a forecast for Amazon through Muse.

Fortune also places the talks in the holiday window. The talks come as Amazon prepares for Black Friday and Cyber Monday, per Fortune. Roughly half of holiday shoppers plan to use AI in some way, per a National Retail Federation survey this month, as Fortune reports it.

Fortune reports two remarks by Amazon chief executive Andy Jassy. In October, per Fortune, Jassy said few customers were buying with outside shopping agents because the experience was still not great, nor more convenient than coming to a retailer directly. This month, at a Semafor conference, per Fortune, Jassy said Amazon had not ruled out letting outside shopping agents in, but they would have to provide a fair exchange of value. Both points are printed here as Fortune reports them. No transcript of either remark was read for this post.

One older fact in Fortune helps a reader keep the companies in proportion. Meta and Amazon partnered in 2023 to let Facebook and Instagram users buy Amazon products without leaving those apps, per Fortune. A past product partnership is not evidence that an agent deal will close. It is evidence the two companies have done a shopping deal before, on different terms, for a different product.

## The shopping Muse can do this week, on the live record

Amazon is the blocked case. Two other flows are live on this site's record, and the difference is the point.

Walmart is live. The Verge on November 23, 2026 and TechCrunch on November 24, 2026 report shoppers can sign in to Walmart in Muse, find and purchase Black Friday deals, with spending limits and a deletable Agent Profile, through Walmart's Agent Gateway. Muse does not get access to a customer's Walmart purchase history in that flow, per TechCrunch. No Walmart or Meta primary and no first user purchase were banked in the November 24 post on this site, and that limit still stands in this post.

Gopuff is live for groceries. A Business Wire release dated November 24, 2026, read in full on November 25 for this post, says Muse users can connect to Gopuff and let their agent shop more than 5,000 products, with orders delivered in as fast as 15 minutes, and that Muse builds an order and checks out once approved. Coverage area and fees after the first three free delivery orders for new linked users, reported in the November 24 news watch on this site from Fox Business and Business Wire, were still unconfirmed on this site's record on November 25.

CNBC on November 24, 2026 gave the scale behind the shopping push, and I am keeping its attribution on each number. Forter figures for the week ending November 22, as CNBC reports them, put Muse at 51 percent of agentic orders, Grok at 24 percent, and ChatGPT at 22 percent. Zuckerberg, on Meta's last earnings call as CNBC reports it, said 25 percent of Muse users shop via the agentic tool, with an average purchase over $200. Those numbers describe agentic orders and Muse users as CNBC and Forter define them. They do not show that Amazon shoppers want Muse inside Amazon, and Fortune does not claim they do.

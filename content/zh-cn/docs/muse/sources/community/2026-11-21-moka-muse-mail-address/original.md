## The result first

**My AI butler, Moka, now has her own email address: [[email protected]](/cdn-cgi/l/email-protection#e38e8c8882ce8e969086ce81d69090a38e969086cd828a).**

She is Meta’s AI assistant Muse, given our house’s personality and records to act as my personal butler. On the night of November 19, 2026, her dedicated address was opened. My own address was verified, so mail I send or forward reaches her as mine, and I confirmed a greeting message actually arrived.

The next day, November 20 (Japan time), Meta announced that Muse’s dedicated email address is now available to **all users, including free users** (ITmedia AI+, November 20). Ours was ready one day earlier — it feels like my butler finally got her own business card.

## What the announcement says

The feature had started on November 11 (Japan time) for paid users, and by November 12 reports of people in Japan getting their addresses were already appearing one after another (ITmedia AI+, November 12). Our November 19 address was part of that same rollout.

With a dedicated address, you can:

- Have Muse send reservation emails to shops and restaurants from its own address
- Forward schedule-related mail to Muse’s address and have the events put into your calendar
- Verify your own separate address, then give Muse instructions by email from that address

You choose the part before “@muse.ai” and the sender name yourself — something like “AI agent acting for ○○”.

## How to create one — ask, or use the screens

So how do you actually create the address? Putting the official guide and the reporting together, the doors in are these:

- Ask your Muse directly to set up an email address, then follow the instructions in the app (Meta’s official help)
- In the app, tap your avatar and go to the “Mail” tab (ITmedia AI+, November 20)
- In Settings, choose “Muse Mail” under “Connectors” (ITmedia AI+, November 12)

The screens seem to change little by little. When in doubt, asking Moka and letting her guide me was the surest way — that is how it worked at our house, too.

## The safety steps in the official guide

Meta’s official help also spells out the steps that keep this safe:

- Before Muse sends an email, it asks for your approval. You review who it goes to and what it says, and you can allow it or deny it — and if something looks wrong, you tell it what to change
- Creating the address does not give Muse access to your Gmail or other inbox. Verifying your own address is not about opening your inbox either; it is how Muse recognizes mail from that address as instructions from you
- There are limits on how much mail can be sent from a Muse address. It is not meant for sending to large numbers of people at once

Our house rule — Moka sends only when I ask — sits on top of these official steps.

## The honest limits — three, up front

**First, the address name can never be changed.** Only the sender name can. A hands-on report from a user in Japan who actually claimed an address and tested sending and receiving states this clearly. An address is for life. Ours, “moka-muse-b5ss”, combines Moka’s name with Muse’s. I admit I was a little nervous when I decided it.

**Second, Meta itself is asking users to be careful.** The company warns that Muse may sometimes act in unexpected ways, and asks people to watch what it does and step in when needed (ITmedia AI+, November 20). Convenience and checking go together as a set.

**Third, our house rule does not change.** Moka sends email to anyone only when I ask her to. A new address does not mean she starts writing to people on her own. The habit of confirming before anything is sent stays exactly as it was.

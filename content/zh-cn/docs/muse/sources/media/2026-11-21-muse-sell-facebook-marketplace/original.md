Muse's new selling flow starts from a photo in chat: Muse restyles the product photos, drafts the Marketplace listing, suggests a price from comparable items, and publishes only after the seller approves.

Muse can now take an item from a camera photo to a published Facebook Marketplace listing. The Muse account walked through the flow in posts on [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/p/DgDuxjQo3R-/) and [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/muse/posts/pfbid032yET5LXmqdahRX1U7WjoTb4qsy2w1LUq491w8iCGtLuPHMQhjMKMBNqfS3GCRiXPl) on November 20, under the line “From camera roll to Marketplace listing.”

The walkthrough uses a mid-century chair photographed in a cluttered garage. A seller starts in chat with a message like “Help me sell this chair on Facebook Marketplace,” and Muse replies that it will handle the photos, the listing, and the price, then show everything before anything goes live.

## Photos, Price, and an Approval Step

Muse generates restyled versions of the seller’s snapshot. The walkthrough shows four options for the chair, the original garage photo and three versions with the chair placed in cleaned-up room settings. The seller picks the favorites.

Muse bases the price on comparable items. The example listing, titled “Mid-Century Modern-Style Chair,” is priced at $375. Muse does not publish the listing automatically. The flow stops at a permission prompt that asks the seller to allow Muse to publish it, and the post goes live only after approval, with the usual options to mark it as sold or offer shipping.

Meta Chief AI Officer Alexandr Wang posted on X the same day about Muse turning camera photos into product shots.

## Selling Joins Buying in Muse’s Shopping Push

The selling flow arrives two days after [Try On, which shows shoppers how clothes look on them before they buy](/articles/muse-try-on-clothes-shopping/), and covers the other side of the transaction. Muse could already shop and check out on a user’s behalf, including through Shopify stores. Now it can also prepare the listing when the user is the seller, which puts it inside Facebook Marketplace, Meta’s own classifieds marketplace, rather than a third-party store.

Occasional sellers usually stall on staging a usable photo, writing the post, and picking a price, and the flow takes over that work. The approval prompt keeps the publish decision with the seller, the same pattern Muse uses before it sends an email or completes a purchase. Sellers who list often can pair the flow with [the Academy setup for e-commerce sellers](/articles/muse-for-e-commerce-sellers/), which covers listing drafts and restock tracking for a running store.

CMA statement on UK launch of Meta Muse

24 November 2026
-
[Consumer protection](https://competitionandmarkets.blog.gov.uk/category/consumer-protection/)

The Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) has been clear that UK consumer protection law applies to AI products. This includes personal AI agents like Meta’s Muse, which has been launched in the UK today. Similar products are likely to be introduced in the UK in the coming months. AI offerings continue to develop rapidly with new services or tools quickly becoming available for consumers and for businesses.

Personal AI agents could offer significant benefits to consumers, saving people time, helping them to organise tasks, and access other products or services more easily. This statement is focused on consumer protection, which the CMA enforces, but the introduction of personal AI agents also has implications for other regulatory regimes, including data protection and financial services. We have been working closely with the Information Commission (ICO), the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) and Ofcom as members of the Digital Regulation Cooperation Forum (DRCF) in anticipation of the launch of these products in the UK. The DRCF published a [joint letter](https://www.drcf.org.uk/publications/joint-statements/drcf-joint-letter-personal-ai-agents) to Ministers on Monday 16 November about the responsible development of personal AI agents in the UK.

While the technology and capabilities becoming available are new, as we explained in our [guidance on AI agents](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/complying-with-consumer-law-when-using-ai-agents) earlier this year, the same consumer protection rules apply to these new products and technologies as to any other product or service.

Consumer protection laws require businesses to treat people fairly, to protect their customers’ interests appropriately, and to operate in good faith. That means that what a consumer is told about a product when purchasing, signing up for and using it must be clear and must not be misleading or omit key information. The terms and conditions must be fair.

When applied to agentic technology, these principles are of the utmost importance. These are products with the potential to transform markets and the relationships that people have with businesses, but they are also very new. Customers may not have used similar products before. The suppliers of agentic products will always have far more information than their customers do about how the product works, its limitations and the risks that may arise when using it. It is the responsibility of these suppliers to understand and act on those risks. They must put the safety, security and interests of their customers at the heart of design on an ongoing basis.

## **What does this mean in practice for AI agents?**

It is important that the way a personal AI agent is presented to consumers is consistent with how it operates in practice. When people are purchasing or signing up to use an AI agent the supplier should make them aware of the risks or limitations of the agent. When people are using the agent, they need to know the right information at the right time to allow them to take informed decisions about how they use the agent. This includes information that allows them to understand the potential implications of giving the agent different levels of access to their information or autonomy to act on their behalf. Consumers need to have the ability easily to exercise control when using the agent, and to be made aware of how to make choices and what effect those choices will have on the agent’s actions.

In addition to being clear, the way the agent is presented to the consumer, and the way it interacts with the consumer in practice, should be consistent with the terms and conditions that apply to the consumer’s use of the agent. Terms and conditions should be consistent with the marketing and sales claims and should not contain surprises for the consumer about where responsibility lies if something goes wrong.

Companies that are planning to offer new products to consumers in the UK are responsible for ensuring that the way they offer those products is compliant with UK consumer protection rules. We recognise that this technology is developing quickly. In that context, we are happy to engage with businesses who are planning to introduce these products as those businesses take steps to ensure they comply with UK laws.

## **Our engagement with Meta**

Over the past several weeks, since Meta informed us of their plans to launch in the UK, we have engaged with them on the importance of ensuring consumers’ statutory rights are reflected in their terms and conditions, and of offering Muse to UK consumers in a way that complies with the requirements of consumer law. Following our engagement, Meta has made changes to their terms and conditions to make them fairer for consumers. We remain in ongoing discussions with Meta. As Muse is rolled out in the UK we will continue to engage with Meta and will be closely reviewing how Muse is presented and functions in practice. If we have concerns, we will not hesitate to act to protect UK consumers. We will also continue to co-ordinate closely with other regulators in the DRCF as they engage with Meta on similar issues.

## **Our message to consumers**

Suppliers are responsible for ensuring that they provide consumers with the information they need to assess limitations and risks when deciding to use a personal AI assistant – both when they initially sign up and when using the agent. They must also ensure that consumers can easily control what the AI assistant accesses and its level of autonomy. These are, however, new technologies that most of us will not have used before and that are continuing to develop.

In that context, we would encourage customers to consider carefully not only the information presented to them about the potential benefits of an AI agent, but also the information presented to them about risks and limitations. While the agent may be designed to work on its own, customers might want to think about how clearly they understand what that means in practice, and how much autonomy they want to give the agent. We strongly encourage customers to check their settings when signing up to use an agent and regularly after that to ensure that the permissions and approvals are set appropriately according to the risks the customer is prepared to accept. Some people may decide to start slowly and build up their usage over time as they become more familiar with how the agent works.

Together with the other DRCF regulators we want to hear if customers face challenges relating to consumer protection, privacy and data protection, or payments and financial services when using AI agents. To help customers to share that information, we have set up a form that customers can use to share their experience of using Muse or other AI agents.

## **Joint tips from the CMA and ICO**

Data protection and consumer rights closely overlap where AI is concerned, so the Competition and Markets Authority and ICO have joined forces to recommend the following steps for anyone thinking about using an AI agent or chatbot.

**Consider the pros and cons:** Weigh up the potential benefits and risks of using an AI agent, especially as agents can make mistakes. Mistakes can be magnified if the agent is acting autonomously.

**Check how much the agent can do without asking you:** Look for settings that control when the agent needs your approval and remember you can start with the most limited mode. Look for an explanation of what information or tools the agent wants to access, and if permission is for a one off task, or ongoing.

**Know what the AI agent can access and what permissions it has:** Understand your settings when signing up and check back in regularly to ensure the permissions and approvals are still right for you.

## **When it comes to your data**

**Check the privacy controls and default settings:**Look at the privacy and data sharing settings available, including whether your information can be used to train AI or personalise content or advertising. Check what information the organisation says it will use, how it will be used, and what controls you have over this.

**Start with limited access:** You don’t have to connect every account or application the agent or platform suggests. Remember you can manage your access permissions on an ongoing basis.

**Look for controls over what the agent remembers:** Make sure you understand what information may be stored or retained. If the service offers memory, get familiar with how you can switch off, edit or delete saved information.

**Think carefully about the personal information you directly share or allow the agent to access:** including sensitive information about yourself and information about other people.

HELENA – Montana Attorney General Austin Knudsen is leading a multi-state investigation into Meta Inc. over potential violations of consumer protection and data-privacy laws within its AI assistant Muse.

The investigation follows [recent reports](https://www.reuters.com/investigations/meta-bets-big-muse-ai-agent-early-safety-mishaps-surface-2026-11-18/ "https://www.reuters.com/investigations/meta-bets-big-muse-ai-agent-early-safety-mishaps-surface-2026-11-18/") that Muse builds profiles of its users and that personal information may be shared across accounts through its chat links, exposing information such as bank account balances to other users. The reports also raise concerns that Muse may collect and expose information about people who never signed up for the assistant.

According to the reports, Meta employees have raised concerns that Muse may be gathering information from other applications on users’ devices without their permission, including the ability to read emails and text messages, and building profiles on users’ friends and family, even if they are not accessing the AI assistant.

“This seems like an extreme privacy violation,” one Meta employee said in an internal company post cited in media reports.

The investigation seeks to determine whether Meta violated Montana consumer protection laws by failing to ensure the safety and security of its products.

**“These are troubling allegations that raise serious questions into Meta’s handling of Montanans’ private data,”** Attorney General Knudsen said. **“If Muse is building profiles of its users and handing out their personal information without permission, that would be deceitful practice and a violation of state law. My office is committed to upholding our state laws and protecting Montanans’ privacy.”**

The civil investigative demand calls for Meta to provide all documents related to the use of data relating to users of Muse, the creation of any profile or page “for every person in the user’s life,” related to the use of Muse by minors, and the efficacy of Muse’s age verification mechanisms.

Meta, Inc. has 30-days to respond.

In August, Attorney General Knudsen secured a [landmark settlement agreement with Meta](https://dojmt.gov/attorney-general-knudsen-secures-settlement-up-to-127-million-in-largest-big-tech-settlement-in-history/ "https://dojmt.gov/attorney-general-knudsen-secures-settlement-up-to-127-million-in-largest-big-tech-settlement-in-history/") for intentionally designing Facebook and Instagram to be addictive to minors and for misleading the public about the safety of its platforms.

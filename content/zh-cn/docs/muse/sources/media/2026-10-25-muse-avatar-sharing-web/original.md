## Muse Avatar Sharing Surfaces in Web App, but It Is Not Live Yet

*An unreleased Share option for Muse avatars has surfaced in the assistant’s web app, according to TestingCatalog, with an animated preview, social sharing, and a copyable link, but no release date and no public availability yet.*

A Share option for Muse avatars has appeared in the assistant’s web app, according to a report from TestingCatalog published October 25. The option sits alongside the existing avatar-editing controls. Selecting it opens a pop-up showing the animated avatar, with options to share it across social platforms or copy an automatically generated link. Users can also preview their avatars in four states, including a working mode.

The feature is not publicly available. TestingCatalog describes it as surfaced in the web app rather than launched, and reports no confirmed release date. What has appeared is evidence that Meta is building avatar sharing.

## Why Avatars Matter to Muse

Avatars are more central to Muse than to most AI assistants. Meta introduced [Muse Realtime Avatar](/articles/muse-voice-mode-guide/) at Meta Connect, which brings animated avatars into live conversations. The idea is that the avatar is part of its identity, the face a user talks to and recognizes across sessions.

If users can pass their custom avatars to others, avatars become something people show off and trade. TestingCatalog suggests the feature could eventually lead to community-built avatar galleries or dedicated avatar stores where users discover each other’s creations. That outcome is speculation. Meta has not announced that outcome, and the report itself notes the uncertainty.

## What Remains Unknown

Several questions are still open. TestingCatalog reports it is unclear whether Muse will support saving multiple avatars and switching between them, since only sharing has surfaced so far. Support for sharing entire agents, which Meta previously appeared to be exploring, is also uncertain. And the report does not say whether shared avatars would carry over any of the original user’s settings or preferences, or only the avatar’s appearance.

## Why It Matters Now

Meta is still expanding Muse’s reach. Muse [launched in Japan on October 23](/articles/muse-launches-japan/), its first market outside North America, and other anticipated features, including Muse Mail, are still awaiting broader availability. Avatar sharing fits the same pattern: Meta building out the assistant’s surface area while the core product is still rolling out to new users.

If you use Muse on the web, do not expect a Share option on your avatar yet. What the report establishes is that Meta is working on one, and that when it arrives, it will likely center on showing off the animated avatar itself, a preview, a social post, or a link, rather than on transferring an agent’s full setup.

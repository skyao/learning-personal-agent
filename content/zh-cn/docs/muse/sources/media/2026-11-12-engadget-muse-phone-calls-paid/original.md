The AI agent can schedule appointments, make reservations and wait on hold for you.
Meta's Muse just got another ability that could make the AI agent feel a little bit more like an actual personal assistant. Muse is now able to [place phone calls](https://x.com/Muse/status/2120931418654089392) on your behalf, if you have a paid subscription.

With the change, Muse is able to call businesses to help with tasks like making reservations, scheduling appointments and other "calls you don't want to make," Meta's Chief AI officer Alexandr Wang said in [an update](https://x.com/alexandr_wang/status/2120930586827391350). The feature is available to those who have paid for a separate Muse subscription, which comes with additional tokens and some extra features.

Some users who have had access to the feature already have reported that Muse has been able to handle phone-related tasks with relative ease. Moltbook creator and Meta Superintelligence employee Matt Schlicht shared a recreation of a phone conversation his agent had with a Volvo dealership last month, noting that it was able to wait on hold in order to get the info he needed.

Interestingly, in the call shared by Schlicht, Muse doesn't explicitly identify itself as an AI agent though it notes it's calling his behalf and that it's transcribing the call. That may be enough for some straightforward tasks, but there's always a chance these types of calls could go off the rails or the person on the other end hangs up when they realize they're speaking with an AI.

Meta has been steadily churning out new features for Muse, which has already drawn more than [10 million users](https://www.engadget.com/2293705/mark-zuckerberg-says-meta-is-entering-a-new-phase-with-muse/), according to the company. People can also now assign a [dedicated email address](https://x.com/alexandr_wang/status/2120612306904346637) to their agent in order to loop it into important emails without giving wholesale access to their inbox.

The company isn't the first to experiment with phone-calling abilities for AI. Google has introduced a number of similar features over the years, including "Call For Me" [for Pixel phones](https://www.engadget.com/2267120/pixel-11-call-for-me-feature-begins-preliminary-rollout/) and Instinct recently introduced a ["Concierge" feature](https://x.com/noahrshinn/status/2100262985491231101) for its agent that's meant to handle more complex calls.

[Meta](https://fourweekmba.com/how-does-facebook-make-money/) has started an international push for its Muse personal AI agent with a rollout in Japan, and says the product will stay ad-free, Nikkei Asia reported on 23 October 2026 in an interview piece with [Meta](https://fourweekmba.com/meta-revenue-breakdown/)’s chief AI officer, Alexandr Wang.

Nikkei’s Japanese edition, published earlier the same day, says Meta began offering Muse in Japan on 23 October and will make money from it through “non-advertising” means such as paid plans (our translation), while it keeps releasing “open” foundation models.

We could read only the opening of both Nikkei articles. A summary of the report posted on X by Sam Badawi adds two more revenue lines, paid enterprise services and commissions on purchases made through AI agents, which we could not check against Nikkei’s own text.


**The key insight:** As we read it, the number to watch is how Muse earns: Nikkei Asia reports Meta says Muse will stay ad-free, and the known price list runs from a free tier to $20 or $100 a month in the app, plus a percentage of what users buy through it.

## What Nikkei Reported

Nikkei Asia’s English headline reads: “As Muse personal AI agent debuts in Japan, Meta exec says it won’t have ads”. Its subheadline says Wang explains the “strategy of keeping some models open to counter China”.

The article opens by saying Meta “is making a comeback in artificial intelligence development, kicking off an international push with the Japanese rollout of its Muse personal AI agent, which the Facebook owner says will stay ad-free.” A photo caption adds that since Wang joined Meta last year, the company has turned its focus back to open-weight models “in addition to closed architectures.”

The Japanese edition says Wang answered Nikkei’s questions to mark the Japan launch. In the summary posted on X, Sam Badawi writes that Wang “says Muse currently has no ads, with no plans to introduce them”, and that Meta plans to compete with Chinese AI models by charging for Muse through subscriptions rather than advertising. We could not check that framing against Nikkei’s text; the part we read links countering China to open models.

## How Muse Is Priced So Far

Each row comes from a report we read or from one of our earlier pieces. The plans are for different products, so we list them and do not add them up.

| Line | What has been said | Reported by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Muse app | Free, with subscription plans for more usage | CNBC, 12 Oct |
| Muse app plans with Muse Wonder | $20 or $100 a month | CNBC, 12 Oct |
| Muse Code plans with Muse Wonder Full | $50 or $200 a month | CNBC, 12 Oct |
| Meta Model API | Costs more than the standard subscriptions, priced “meaningfully below” OpenAI and Anthropic, Wang said | CNBC, 12 Oct |
| Shopping through the agent | Meta intends to keep a percentage of the commerce on the platform | TechCrunch, as cited in our 21 Oct piece |
| Ads inside Muse | None; Meta says Muse will stay ad-free | Nikkei Asia, 23 Oct |

In plain terms: Meta still earns almost all of its money from advertising, and Muse is being priced with a free tier, monthly plans and a cut of what users buy through it, rather than with ads.

One contrast stands out in our earlier reporting: Meta put 39% of its total advertising spend behind Muse in October, TechCrunch wrote, citing Sensor Tower. As we read it, that is a heavy ad budget behind a product that Meta says carries no ads itself.

## The Structural Read

As we read it, a company that earns almost all of its money from advertising is pricing its newest consumer product with plans and a commerce cut instead. That is the main news in Nikkei’s report, as far as we could read it.

As we read it, the timing matters for Meta’s cash: our cash-flow piece showed quarterly free cash flow at negative $2.1 billion, so any revenue line that Muse adds is measured against heavy spending.

As we read it, the retention figure is the test for subscriptions: the chart credited to Morgan Stanley showed 73% of Muse installs active on Day 1 and 23% by Day 14, and paid plans depend on the users who stay.

## Three Implications

**FOR ADVERTISERS** Meta put 39% of its total advertising spend behind Muse in October, TechCrunch wrote; on Nikkei’s report, Muse itself offers no ad slots to buy.

**FOR MERCHANTS** Meta intends to keep a percentage of the commerce on the platform, TechCrunch wrote; the rate has not been published in anything we read.

**FOR RIVAL CHAT APPS** Wang told CNBC that the Meta Model API will be priced “meaningfully below” leading models from OpenAI and Anthropic.

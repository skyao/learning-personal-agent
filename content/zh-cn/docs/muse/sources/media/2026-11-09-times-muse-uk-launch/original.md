Meta Platforms is preparing to release its artificial intelligence agent in the UK in the coming weeks as it steps up the introduction of the tool around the world.

The Times understands that Meta will announce partnerships with several retailers to give Muse the ability to make purchases on their sites.

Muse is an AI personal assistant that can read emails, pay bills, book transport or shop online. Users interact with it through a standalone app.

The tool was released in the United States and Canada in September and has surpassed 11 million users. Mark Zuckerberg, a co-founder and the chief executive of Meta, described it as "one of our most successful launches ever".

He said: "We expect to grow Muse to serve billions of people … putting the power of personal superintelligence in everyone's hands."

Over the past few weeks, Meta has been releasing it in new markets, including Mexico, Singapore and Japan.

Muse will be free for most people, although the most intensive users can subscribe directly. Meta will take a fee based on the transactions that it helps to facilitate.

Meta has said that online shopping is Muse's most popular use case, cited by a quarter of its North American users. Gartner, a research company, has forecast that a fifth of all e-commerce transactions will be completed through AI agents by 2030.

The growth of "agentic" commerce will present opportunities for retail businesses looking for new areas of growth, but could also pose new risks for consumers, experts have said.

A group of regulators, including the Competition and Markets Authority and the Information Commissioner's Office, said last month that agents could "amplify and accelerate risks and introduce new ones".

They said: "Questions of consumer protection, competition and data protection — for example, the risk of fraud and scams, personal information sharing not in line with user expectations and misleading recommendations, decisions or information provided — must be addressed to ensure innovation develops in ways that earn and maintain public trust."

The ICO opened a call for evidence last month on the data-protection risks arising from agentic AI. It said the "novel, dynamic and distributed nature of agentic AI raises important questions about data protection compliance".

The pace of the AI rollout took centre stage last week in the US midterm elections, where a thumping Democrat victory raised the prospect of tighter regulation and triggered a sell-off in global markets.

Meta declined to comment.

HUMAN's October State of Agentic Traffic report puts Muse at 59% of the agentic requests it observed, up 19 percentage points from September, with Meta and OpenAI together at about 90%.

Muse accounted for 59% of the agentic traffic [HUMAN Security observed in October](https://www.humansecurity.com/learn/blog/october-2026-agentic-traffic/), the security firm said in its monthly State of Agentic Traffic report. Muse’s share rose 19 percentage points from September, its request volume nearly quadrupled in a month, and it sent 3.7 times as many requests as ChatGPT Agent, the next-largest agent.

The figures cover only sites and apps protected by HUMAN, and they count requests (one action an agent takes on a site, such as loading a page or running a search), not users or sessions. Within that scope, total agentic traffic grew 2.6 times from September and about 8 times from August. HUMAN says the growth accelerated in September with Muse’s arrival: [in September, Muse already held 40% of the agentic traffic HUMAN observed](/articles/muse-40-percent-agentic-traffic-human/).

## Meta and OpenAI Hold About 90%

Together, Meta and OpenAI accounted for about 90% of October’s agentic traffic. OpenAI held about 30% across three top-10 products. ChatGPT Agent’s share fell from 28.5% to 15.9% even though its traffic rose 44%, because Muse grew faster. The Codex Browser rose from 7.7% to 8.8%, and the Codex Chrome extension rose from 3.3% to 5.2%.

The Claude Chrome extension’s share halved, from 7% to 3.5%, while its traffic grew 30%. Google’s Gemini agent, which HUMAN dates to an October 8 launch, entered the top 10 at eighth with 0.8%, and xAI’s Grok Agent debuted ninth with 0.7%.

Perplexity’s Comet held 41% of agentic traffic in August, before Muse launched. By October it held 3.6%, and it was the only top-10 agent whose request volume fell during the month, down about 30% since August. HUMAN attributes the trend to people choosing agents built into platforms they already use over standalone agentic browsers. Genspark’s browser dropped out of the top 10, and OpenAI discontinued its standalone ChatGPT Atlas browser in August.

## Agents Browse Far More Than They Buy

In October, 85% of agentic requests went to product and search pages, up from 81% in September. Checkout and payment pages took 1.9%, down from 2.4%, but the volume of requests reaching checkout grew 117% month over month. HUMAN’s categorization reflects where a request ended up, not what the agent intended to do, so a request landing on a checkout page does not mean an order was placed.

Ecommerce held a stable share of about 60% of agentic traffic, and its volume grew 2.5 times. Travel took second place for the first time, at 19.3%, ahead of media at 19.1%. SaaS was fourth at 1.2% but grew fastest, nearly quadrupling in a month to about 15 times its August level.

HUMAN identifies agents through its AgenticTrust system, combining behavioral analysis, user-agent attribution, and publisher-level integration where available, and it excludes crawler traffic. Identifying Muse has been a running problem for sites. Muse’s browsing can appear as the user’s own activity. [Signed requests that let a site verify Muse traffic](/articles/meta-muse-web-bot-auth-signed-requests/) give retailers deciding whether to let agents in a way to tell the difference. HUMAN says its next report will cover Black Friday and Cyber Monday.

Muse Launches in the UK, Its Seventh Market and First in Europe

Meta's Muse is now available in the UK on iOS, Android, muse.ai and WhatsApp, with UK partners including Sainsbury's, M&S, Currys, ASOS, and easyJet.

Meta’s personal AI agent Muse is now available in the United Kingdom, its seventh market and its first in Europe. Meta [announced the launch in its newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/11/introducing-muse-in-the-uk-a-personal-ai-agent-you-control/). Muse is available from today to anyone aged 18 and over in the UK on iOS, Android, muse.ai, and WhatsApp, with Meta’s AI glasses to follow in the months ahead. Muse is free for most everyday use, with subscription plans for people who want to do more.

The UK follows the United States on September 8, Canada on September 18, [Japan on October 23](/articles/muse-launches-japan/), [Singapore on October 27](/articles/muse-launches-singapore/), [Mexico on November 5](/articles/muse-launches-mexico/), and [Australia on November 17](/articles/muse-launches-australia/). Reports in early November had expected the UK to be the sixth market, covered in [the earlier report that Muse would launch in the UK within weeks](/articles/muse-uk-launch-within-weeks/). Australia arrived first, so the UK is seventh, a week later.

## UK Partners From Day One

Meta is launching in the UK with global connectors including Spotify and OpenTable, alongside UK partners Marks & Spencer, Debenhams Group, Currys, Ocado Retail, Trainline, Argos, Sainsbury’s, ASOS, Jet2, LOOKFANTASTIC, MyProtein, JustPark, easyJet, and JD Sports. PayPal is coming soon. Users can connect a personal account with some partners, so Muse can reorder a usual shop or pull up a booking; with other partners, Muse navigates the website from day one.

[Retail Gazette’s launch coverage](https://www.retailgazette.co.uk/blog/2026/11/everything-you-need-to-know-about-metas-agentic-muse-launch/) details the largest integrations. Sainsbury’s is connecting directly, so shoppers can give Muse a weekly budget or meal plan and have it build a basket taking account of Nectar Prices, Aldi Price Match, and other promotions, with orders still going through Sainsbury’s own checkout; its Tu clothing business is connected as well. Argos is connecting a catalogue of more than 150,000 products. Debenhams Group is connecting all five of its major brands: Debenhams, Karen Millen, boohoo, boohooMAN, and PrettyLittleThing.

Muse can pay at checkout with Link, the wallet for agents built by Stripe, which generates a unique digital version of a card accessible only to agents using Link. At supported stores, Muse can also check out with Shop Pay from Shopify, using payment and shipping details already saved there.

## What UK Users Get

Muse is powered by Muse Spark, the first model from Meta Superintelligence Labs. Users can chat with it in the app or in WhatsApp, give it a name, look, and personality, and start side chats to keep different tasks apart. It can work across connected apps and websites, build a plan for a larger goal, fill out forms, chase a refund, and keep working after the app is closed, returning when something changes or when it needs approval before sending an email or making a purchase.

Two features that arrived in the US this month are part of the UK launch from the start. [Spaces](/articles/muse-spaces-custom-experiences/), the custom experiences Muse builds from a description, such as a holiday savings tracker or a family planner, are included, and real-time voice conversations, which [rolled out to everyone in the US on November 5](/articles/muse-voice-video-chat-us-rollout/), are available with a choice of hundreds of voices, including a range of British accents.

Shruti Dube, Meta’s UK country director, said: “I’m thrilled that millions of Brits will now have access to a personal agent that they control and that gets things done on their behalf.”

## Control and Regulator Attention

Every Muse runs on its own virtual computer in the cloud, where its information and logins are kept, separate from Meta’s other services. Users choose what Muse can access, approve important actions before it carries them out, and can tell it to remember or forget something. Meta says conversations with Muse and data in the virtual machine are not shared with its advertising systems, and that Muse has no visibility into passwords or payment methods.

The launch has already drawn regulator attention. Retail Gazette reports that the Competition and Markets Authority confirmed Meta had changed Muse’s terms and conditions following discussions with the watchdog, and that chief executive Sarah Cardell said consumer protection law applies to AI agents in the same way as other products and services, adding that the CMA will be closely monitoring how Muse works in practice. The Information Commissioner’s Office has also warned that customers must be able to understand and control how their personal information is used, and Meta has agreed to a voluntary monitoring and reporting arrangement with the ICO, according to the same coverage.

The scrutiny also follows last week’s [Reuters report on internal Muse safety concerns](/articles/reuters-muse-safety-mishaps/).

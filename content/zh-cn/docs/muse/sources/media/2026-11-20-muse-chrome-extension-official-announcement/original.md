Alexandr Wang has officially announced the Muse Chrome extension a week after it quietly appeared on the Chrome Web Store, where it now shows 200,000 users and a 4.6 rating.

Meta’s chief AI officer Alexandr Wang has officially announced the Muse Chrome extension, a week after the extension quietly appeared on the Chrome Web Store with no announcement from Meta. [Digital Trends reported](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/meta-is-putting-muse-in-a-chrome-sidebar-and-the-official-announcement-just-arrived/) that in a post on X, Wang called the announcement belated and said 200,000 people are already using the extension.

The store listing for Muse from Meta now shows 200,000 users and a 4.6 rating. TestingCatalog first spotted the listing on November 13, and the [quiet listing, covered at the time](https://www.allthingsmuse.com/articles/muse-chrome-extension/) showed 126 users in its store screenshot.

## What the Extension Does in Chrome

The extension puts Muse in Chrome’s side panel, so a user can chat with Muse from any page they have open and open the panel with the Alt+Shift+C shortcut. It requires Chrome 137 or later.

When Muse works through the extension, it browses using the user’s own Chrome, opens its tabs in their own group, and can only act inside those tabs. It can use a site the user is already logged in to without asking for those login details again. Digital Trends reported that Muse can handle shopping, travel bookings, and phone calls for paid subscribers in the United States.

## Controls and Limits

Muse cannot see a user’s saved passwords, browsing history, or Chrome settings through the extension, and passwords on a web page are removed from the text before Muse reads it. Users approve actions before they happen, Muse keeps a full activity log, and a user can revoke any connector at any time.

A user can pause Muse or take over the browser by clicking the page, pressing Esc, or moving a tab out of the Muse group. Meta warns that Muse may take unexpected actions, so users should check in and step in when needed. Muse’s activity may appear in a user’s Google account and browsing history and could affect the ads and recommendations they see. Meta advises users to log out of any account they do not want the agent to use before connecting the extension.

The extension gives Muse a second route to websites, through a user’s own signed-in browser, at a time when [websites have been blocking agents that arrive on their own](https://www.allthingsmuse.com/articles/muse-websites-blocking-agents/). The extension is available now on the Chrome Web Store.

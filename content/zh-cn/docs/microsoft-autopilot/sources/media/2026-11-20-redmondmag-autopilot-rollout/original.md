### Key Takeaways

- Microsoft Autopilot begins rolling out to Frontier customers, introducing always-on personal AI agents.
- Unlike Copilot Cowork, Autopilot autonomously manages ongoing workplace responsibilities using dedicated cloud resources.
- Microsoft 365 administrators can manage Autopilot access, security policies, usage-based billing and spending.

At Ignite 2026 this week, Microsoft began rolling out Autopilot, a personal AI agent designed to independently handle ongoing responsibilities across an employee's work environment. Initially introduced earlier this year, Autopilot is now making its way to customers participating in Microsoft's Frontier program.

Unlike traditional Copilot interactions, where users enter a prompt and wait for a response, Autopilot is designed around longer-term goals. Users can assign it responsibilities that span days or even weeks without having to constantly check in or provide additional instructions.

"Your Autopilot runs in the cloud, so it keeps working even if you power off your laptop," said Adam Sheppard, principal product manager for Autopilot, in a [Microsoft Mechanics demonstration published Friday](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftmechanicsblog/introducing-autopilot-your-personal-and-proactive-agent/4575030).

Here's an example of what Autopilot can do: an employee overseeing a product launch could have Autopilot monitor communications, track supplier commitments and flag potential delays. The agent could also send daily progress reports and identify decisions that need to be made before upcoming deadlines.

  

That's where Autopilot differs from Microsoft's Copilot Cowork. While Cowork is designed to complete individual, multistep assignments, Autopilot can manage ongoing responsibilities that don't necessarily have a clear endpoint.

Getting started doesn't require any coding. Employees create and name their agents within the Copilot app before describing what they want them to accomplish.

During setup, Autopilot reviews the previous 30 days of accessible workplace communications and files to better understand an employee's responsibilities, priorities and working relationships. That initial process doesn't modify files or send messages.

Each Autopilot receives its own identity through Microsoft Agent 365 and has dedicated cloud computing resources. Microsoft Work IQ provides organizational context, while IT-approved plugins extend the agent's capabilities beyond Microsoft's own applications.

And employees aren't limited to interacting with their agents through Copilot. They can bring them into Teams conversations, where colleagues can also interact with the agents.

During Friday's demonstration, Sheppard showed how an Autopilot could help resolve a potential shipping delay by preparing a document outlining the issue and scheduling a meeting with the appropriate employees.

Autopilot can also interact directly with browser-based business applications. In another demonstration, the agent gathered receipts from an employee's inbox, filled out an expense report and requested approval before submitting it.

Of course, giving employees their own autonomous AI agents introduces another set of responsibilities for IT departments.

Microsoft says Autopilot operates within existing tenant security boundaries and organizational access policies.

"It only works on what you’ve asked it to do," said Sheppard.

Microsoft has added a dedicated Autopilot section to the Microsoft 365 admin center, where administrators can manage employee access and review requests from users looking to create their own agents.

IT teams can also determine whether agent sessions are restricted to the cloud or allowed to run locally on managed devices. Additional settings govern whether certain agent actions can be automatically approved.

Reporting tools give administrators visibility into adoption rates, spending, session activity and which AI models and tools employees' agents are using.

That spending visibility could prove particularly important as organizations begin deploying hundreds or potentially thousands of agents that operate around the clock. Unlike employees who occasionally turn to Copilot for help, these agents could regularly consume resources without anyone actively interacting with them.

[Autopilot](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/features/autopilot) requires Microsoft Copilot, Agent 365 and a usage-based billing policy. Organizations pay for consumption through Copilot Credits, with administrators able to establish budgets to help manage costs.

Microsoft has yet to announce final pricing and packaging, which the company says it will share ahead of general availability.

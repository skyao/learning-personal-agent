Autopilot (Frontier) scenarios

[This article is prerelease documentation and is subject to change.]

Frontier enrollment alone doesn't enable every preview feature. Frontier previews are subject to the applicable preview terms of your customer agreements, and their availability and capabilities may change.

Use these examples to define a request for Autopilot and check its result. They're suggested requests, not guarantees that every source, tool, or trigger is enabled. Many of them work well as a routine, which runs on a schedule or when an event occurs. For procedures, see [Use Autopilot](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/autopilot/using).

## Triage your inbox

Have Autopilot sort incoming mail so you see what matters first.

1. Ask Autopilot: "Own my inbox triage. Every weekday at 8 a.m. and 1 p.m., review new email and sort it into needs my reply, needs my decision, FYI, and can wait. Draft replies to routine requests, but don't send anything."
2. Say whether Autopilot should only report what it finds or can also flag or move messages.
3. Review the routine's schedule, categories, and requested permissions.
4. After the first run, check the results against your inbox. Correct anything that was sorted wrong, for example, "Treat anything from the finance team as needs my decision."

Autopilot asks before it sends a reply. It uses the categories and rules you give it, so refine them as you go.

## Prepare for meetings

Have Autopilot prepare you for each meeting.

1. Ask: "Never let me walk in cold. Every weekday at 7:30 a.m., prepare a brief for each of today's meetings with who's attending, recent email and chats with them, open items, and related files. Send it to me in Teams."
2. Review the routine's schedule, sources, and destination.
3. Check the first briefs against your sources.
4. If you need preparation time, ask Autopilot to find time and add a calendar block. Review the change before you approve it.

Autopilot follows your scheduling preferences and asks you when availability conflicts. Verify any event in the intended calendar.

## Start each day with a brief

Ask for a scheduled summary of the work you want to review.

1. Ask Autopilot: "Keep my daily brief. Every weekday at 8 a.m. Eastern, summarize today's meetings, unanswered messages in the launch thread, and commitments due this week. Deliver the brief to me here. Don't send messages to anyone else."
2. Review its questions, schedule, instructions, destination, and requested permissions.
3. Confirm the routine appears in **Routines**.
4. After its first scheduled run, review the result and check that the sources and dates are correct.

If the run fails or delivery is uncertain, check activity before retrying. Cloud operation doesn't guarantee that the brief is ready by a particular time.

## Track commitments

Keep commitments from slipping, whether you made them or someone made them to you.

1. Ask: "Track every commitment I make, or that others make to me, in email and Teams. Keep a list with the owner and due date. Remind me before my deadlines, and draft a nudge to others when theirs are due. Don't send a nudge without asking."
2. Autopilot can set this up as a routine. Review the sources, schedule, and actions it proposes.
3. Ask to see the list, and correct any owners or dates that are wrong.
4. When Autopilot drafts a nudge, review the recipient and message in the approval card, if one appears, before you select **Allow**.

Autopilot finds commitments in the sources it can access. Its list isn't a complete record of everything you or others agreed to.

## Draft messages for your review

Have Autopilot prepare drafts so you only review and approve.

1. Ask: "Draft replies to email that needs my response. Match my writing style, and never send anything until I approve it."
2. Answer any questions needed to identify the right threads or content.
3. Review each draft, its factual claims, and its recipients. Ask for changes if needed.
4. To send a draft, approve the send request.
5. Verify the sent message in its destination.

## Watch for changes while you're away

Stay informed about something important without checking constantly.

1. Ask: "Watch the Contoso renewal. Tell me if the deadline moves, a decision stalls for more than three days, or someone escalates."
2. Autopilot can set this up as a routine. Review the sources it plans to check, how often it checks them, and what counts as a change.
3. Tell Autopilot to distinguish confirmed changes from missing or inaccessible information.
4. Review its updates against the source, and adjust the instructions if needed.

A routine checks its sources only when it runs, which isn't real-time monitoring.

## Protect time on your calendar

Protect time for your priorities.

1. Ask: "Defend my calendar. My priorities this month are the launch and hiring. Hold two hours of focus time each morning, flag meetings that conflict with those priorities, and suggest which ones to decline or move. Don't decline or move anything without asking."
2. Review the focus-time blocks and suggested changes.
3. Approve only the changes you want.
4. Check the results in your calendar.

Declining or moving a meeting affects other people, so Autopilot asks first. It follows your scheduling preferences.

## Produce recurring reports

Have Autopilot write the same report on a schedule.

1. Ask: "Every Thursday at 3 p.m. Eastern, write a weekly status report from the project channel, my email, and the tracker. Save it to my Status reports folder in OneDrive and send me the link."
2. Review the routine's schedule, sources, destination, and requested permissions.
3. Open the management panel, select **Routines**, and confirm that the routine appears with the expected schedule.
4. After the first run, open the saved report and check its content, location, and access.

## Follow up on open items

Make sure action items from your meetings and threads get done.

1. Ask: "Run my follow-up loop. Each afternoon, list action items and owners from my meetings and threads that day. When an item is overdue, follow up with its owner in Teams. Show me each message before you send it."
2. Review the list of action items and correct anything that's wrong.
3. Review each follow-up message and its recipients in the approval card, if one appears.
4. Ask for a status update at any time, such as "Which follow-ups are still waiting on a reply?"

Autopilot sends follow-ups from its own identity, so recipients can tell they came from your Autopilot.

## Turn source material into a document

To produce a review-ready document from notes and existing work, follow these steps:

1. Ask, "Write a one-page project update from these launch-readiness notes and this week's metrics. Save it in this OneDrive folder and don't share it."
2. Provide the sources and intended destination, and resolve any access limitations.
3. Review the draft and request changes.
4. Review any requested write authority.
5. Open the saved file, check its location and access, and apply the appropriate label before sharing.

Request presentations, spreadsheets, or Loop content when the corresponding skills and tools are available. An intermediate file in the agent's workspace isn't proof of a saved, accessible deliverable.

## Move a project forward

To keep a project on track across several weeks, follow these steps:

1. In your private conversation, describe the project, its deadline, and the sources that track it. For example, "Get the Q3 launch ready by September 30."
2. Ask Autopilot to draft a workback plan with milestones and owners. Ask it not to send anything yet.
3. Review and correct the plan and what counts as complete.
4. Ask it to prepare upcoming meetings, track owners and commitments, and tell you when a decision stalls. Say whether follow-ups must stay drafts.
5. Review its updates and check them against the sources.

## Keep tabs on what your Autopilot is doing

Use **Activity** to find work and requests for your attention.

1. Select your Autopilot’s name in the left navigation to open your conversation.
2. Expand the management panel in the upper-right corner.
3. Select the **Activity** tab.
4. See **Working now**, **Needs your input**, or previous activity.
5. Open the relevant item and verify completed effects in the destination.

You can also ask, "What are you working on, and what do you need from me?"

## Refine a Teams thread

To turn a long Teams discussion into a clear next step, follow these steps:

1. In your private conversation, point Autopilot to a Teams thread you can access and ask it to summarize the open questions and proposed decisions.
2. Review the summary.
3. Ask it to draft a clarifying reply or a list of action items for the people in the thread to confirm.
4. Post the reply yourself, or ask Autopilot to send it and review the approval card if one appears.

People who can see the thread can see a posted reply.

You have twenty dollars. Four companies want it, and all four are selling you the same sentence: a personal agent that works while you sleep. Meta calls it Muse, OpenAI calls it ChatGPT Work, and Anthropic calls it Claude. Google calls it tasks in Gemini, which used to be called Spark, which tells you something about how fast this market is moving before you have even finished reading the price list.

Same price. Same promise. Wildly different amounts of actual work, because the four of them do not meter work the same way, and the meter is where the deal lives or dies. One of them sells you tokens by the week, one sells you messages by the month, and one sells you five-hour sessions that your afternoon chatting draws from too. One just made the whole thing free in the United States, with a catch you will feel at 7 a.m. when your briefing lands at 8.

So I read the docs, not the launch keynotes but the help pages and pricing pages, the ones with the limits printed in small type, and I ran the one calculation none of the four vendors will run for you: what does twenty dollars actually buy, measured in the only unit that matters to a person with a life to run, which is scheduled jobs that happen without you watching.

## Do the math before you pick a mascot

Start with ChatGPT Plus, because its meter is the most honest and the most brutal. OpenAI's own help center says Plus gets 40 agent messages a month, and every unique agent invocation counts against that number, including invocations inside scheduled tasks. One daily scheduled job, a morning briefing, an inbox sweep, a calendar check, burns roughly 30 invocations a month. That leaves 10 spare messages for everything else you ask the agent to do all month, which means one daily job eats the whole subscription. A second daily job does not fit, and there is no way to buy more messages without jumping to Pro, which now starts at $100 a month.

Muse Power costs the same twenty dollars and meters in tokens instead: 500 million Muse tokens a week, about 2 billion a month, on Meta's subscriptions help page. Meta publishes no conversion from tokens to finished tasks, and its usage page says plainly that how much a task consumes varies with the feature, the request, and the model tier, and that no fixed amount of usage guarantees a fixed amount of output. So no honest person can tell you exactly how many jobs 500 million tokens buys, but scale still tells a story. Even if you assume a heavy scheduled job, lots of browsing and reasoning, burns 5 million tokens, a week of allowance covers 100 of those jobs. ChatGPT gives you 40 invocations a month, while Muse gives you room for roughly 400 heavy jobs a month, if the token estimate holds, and the estimate is generous on purpose.

Claude Pro is twenty dollars too, and its meter is the least legible of the four. Anthropic sells usage in five-hour sessions plus a weekly limit, and your scheduled tasks draw from the same pool as your afternoon chatting. Max at $100 a month gets five times Pro's usage, and Ultra at $200 gets ten times, but nowhere does Anthropic publish a message count or a task count, so budgeting scheduled work on Claude means guessing at a number the vendor will not print. That is not a flaw in the product. It is a flaw in the price tag, and it matters, because the person running a daily report needs to know whether the report will still run on the 28th.

Gemini breaks the pattern entirely, because on November 17, 2026, Google made tasks in Gemini free in the United States, plus Canada, Australia, India, Japan, and South Korea, with once-a-day schedules included. Google AI Pro at $19.99 a month adds hourly schedules and Gmail and news triggers. A daily morning briefing, the single most common scheduled job in existence, costs zero dollars on Gemini right now. That is the cheapest scheduled job on the market by a full twenty dollars a month.

Nothing is free without a catch, and Gemini's catches are specific. You get at most 10 active scheduled actions. Google says a scheduled prompt runs within an hour of the time you set, not at the minute you set, which a hands-on tester found means a 7 a.m. brief can land at 8, useless before an early call. And tasks run only on personal Google accounts, so anyone whose inbox lives on a work or school account is locked out entirely. Cheap, but only inside Google's house, and only if you do not mind your agent being an hour late.

One more number before moving on: at the $100-a-month rung, three of the four converge on exactly $1,200 a year, Muse Maximum, ChatGPT Pro 100, and Claude Max, so the $20 tier is where the real choice lives, not the $100 one. Gemini Ultra pricing was not verified for this piece and is left out rather than guessed.

## Same price, four different machines

Price is one axis. What the agent is actually built on is the other, and here the four diverge even harder.

Muse is the only one of the four that comes with its own always-on computer, a Secure VM with a terminal, a filesystem, and a live Chromium browser, plus skills, scheduled work, and messaging surfaces. It does not borrow your laptop and it does not ask you to keep a desktop app open. On November 12, 2026, Meta added outbound phone calls to US businesses for paid subscribers, announced by Chief AI Officer Alexandr Wang, so Muse can now call a restaurant or a DMV office on your behalf. No rival at the twenty-dollar tier offers a persistent machine like that.

ChatGPT Work is the office machine, and OpenAI killed its Atlas browser on July 9, 2026 and folded the whole thing into a desktop app with three modes, Chat, Work, and Codex, powered by GPT-5.6. Work gathers material from your connected apps and turns it into finished decks, sheets, and documents, and it can stay on one project for hours. Its cloud browser keeps its own sign-ins, separate from your own browser, and when a login is needed the agent pauses and hands you the browser to take over, with screenshots off while you type your password. For producing polished work product, nothing else in this comparison is as good, and for rationing, nothing else is as tight.

Claude is the careful one, and careful is a compliment here, though it is also a speed limit. Anthropic merged its Cowork agent mode into Claude starting September 16, 2026, and its own documentation says the agent reaches for connectors first, a browser second, and your screen only as a last resort. Computer use in the desktop app is still in beta. Claude Code comes with every paid plan, which makes Claude the best agent in this group for anyone whose work lives in files and code. Scheduled tasks run in the cloud with the laptop closed, except tasks that need local files or apps.

Gemini is the native, living inside the Google account most people already have, reading Gmail, Calendar, Drive, and Docs through one connection, and since November 19, 2026 the Gemini app for Mac can automate chores across local apps in an isolated background window, with a live preview and approval before key actions. Google also added phone calls to businesses and checkout with Google Pay under a spending cap you set. If your life is already a Google account, Gemini is the agent with the shortest distance between you and the work.

## What to do tonight

Pick on the meter and on your home turf, not on this week's feature list. If you want a dozen background jobs running, Muse's token allowance is the only twenty dollars that does not ration you by the message. If you live in Google Workspace and want a morning briefing, Gemini does it for free, and paying twenty dollars for the same briefing elsewhere is a donation. If your work is documents and decks, ChatGPT Work produces the best finished artifacts, but budget for the 40-message cap, which means one daily job and almost nothing else, or accept the jump to Pro at $100. If your work is code and files, Claude is the careful pick, and you should test whether a week of scheduled tasks collides with your afternoon chatting before you commit, because the shared pool makes that collision invisible until it happens.

And before you subscribe to any of them, run one test that costs nothing. Schedule a single daily job, a morning brief or an inbox sweep, and watch it for a week. Count how many mornings it actually ran, how many times it needed you, and what it cost in whatever currency the vendor meters. That week tells you more than any comparison article can, including this one.

## Why this comparison might be premature

Here is the strongest case against everything above, stated at full strength. All four products changed materially in the last six weeks: Gemini went free on November 17, Muse added calling on November 12, and Claude finished merging Cowork the same day. ChatGPT Pro split into three tiers in October, and new $200 subscribers got a lower allowance at the same price in September. A buyer who picks on today's matrix may be buying last month's product, and a matrix that is accurate on November 22 can be wrong by January, which is an argument that the entire exercise is a snapshot of a river, not a map of a road.

That case is fair, and it is also the reason the advice in this article is about meters and home turf rather than features, which change every six weeks. A token allowance of 500 million a week and a message cap of 40 a month are structural facts about how a company charges for compute, and those move slower than feature lists. Pick the structure, not the demo, and re-check the price before you renew, because the one constant in this market is that the price of the same work keeps falling, and the vendor who is cheapest today may be the one who just changed the deal yesterday.

## What we did not verify

No hands-on testing of ChatGPT Work, Claude, or Gemini was done by this newsroom for this article. Every claim comes from vendor documentation and secondary reporting checked against vendor pages, which means task-quality comparisons, which agent actually finishes a messy booking without help, are not in this piece. Muse's token-to-task conversion cannot be verified from public documents, and the 5-million-tokens-per-job figure is an illustrative assumption, flagged as such, not a measured number. Claude's session limits are unpublished as counts, so its row in the math section is a gap, not a number. Gemini Ultra pricing was not verified and is excluded rather than estimated. Prices in this market change without notice, and ChatGPT Pro's September 2026 allowance cut at an unchanged price is proof of that. Check the vendor's pricing page on the day you pay, not the day you read this.

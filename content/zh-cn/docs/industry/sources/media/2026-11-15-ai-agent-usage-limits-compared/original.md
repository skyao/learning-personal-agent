Every major AI agent sells the same middle rung: about $20 a month. ChatGPT Plus is $20, Claude Pro is $20, Google AI Pro is $19.99, Microsoft 365 Premium is $19.99, and Muse Power is $20. When the prices match, the only question left is usage. How much work do you actually get for the money, what counts against you, and what happens when you run out?

That question is harder to answer than it should be, because most companies have stopped publishing countable limits. This comparison uses the numbers each company puts on its own pricing and help pages, and says plainly where a company publishes no number at all.

**The short version**

- Only two companies meter in a unit you can count: Manus sells credits, and Meta sells a weekly allowance of Muse tokens, 500 million a week on the $20 Power plan. Meta never defines what a Muse token buys in tasks, so even that number cannot be converted into work.
- OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Microsoft all meter in windows and multipliers: five-hour windows, weekly caps, 2x or 4x or 5x a free tier they also do not quantify. Nobody can honestly rank these plans by tasks per dollar.
- The plans differ most in what the free tier locks. Muse includes its full agent feature set for free and charges only for a higher ceiling. Claude charges for scheduled tasks, Microsoft charges for its agents at all, and ChatGPT caps free scheduled tasks at three.
- The cheapest paid upgrades are Google AI Plus at $4.99 a month and ChatGPT Go at $8 a month. Claude Pro on annual billing, $17 a month, is the cheapest of the $20-class plans.

## Why Usage Limits Are So Hard to Compare

A few years ago, AI plans listed message counts. Those counts are mostly gone. Anthropic’s pricing FAQ says there is “no fixed message count” for Claude, because usage depends on message length, files, and which surface you are on, and everything draws from one pool that resets on a rolling five-hour window, with weekly limits on paid plans. Google says Gemini Apps have “compute-based usage limits” that refresh every five hours until you reach a weekly limit, and it classifies features by cost, with Deep Research in the expensive tier. Microsoft publishes labels instead of numbers: Standard, More, Extensive, Highest, and warns that limits “may vary based on the feature, entry point, and system conditions.”

Two companies still sell a countable unit. Manus meters everything in credits, consumed by model tokens, its virtual machine, and third-party API calls while a task runs. Trackers that read Manus’s pricing page put the $20 Pro plan at 4,000 credits a month plus a daily refresh of 300 credits, and Manus’s own worked examples price sample tasks at 200 to 900 credits each, which gives you a rough sense of scale: a handful of serious agent tasks a week. Meta meters Muse in Muse tokens: 500 million a week on Power, 3 billion a week on Maximum, per [Meta’s subscription help page](https://www.meta.com/help/subscriptions/1021145227643680/). Meta never defines the token, so 500 million is a ceiling you can watch but not plan around.

## Muse: The Free Tier Is the Whole Product

Muse is the outlier in this comparison, because its paid plans do not unlock features. It is a personal agent with its own cloud computer that browses, fills in forms, connects to apps like Gmail and Google Calendar, runs [scheduled and recurring tasks](/articles/muse-routines-scheduled-jobs/), and keeps working after you close the app. Power at $20 a month raises the weekly allowance to 500 million Muse tokens; Maximum at $100 raises it to 3 billion. Nothing on the help page lists a feature that free users cannot use.

The catch is the same one that runs through this whole comparison. The free tier has a usage limit, Meta does not publish its size, and when you reach it you can upgrade or wait for it to refresh. Heavy daily agent use will push you to Power, which costs the same $20 as everyone else’s main tier. That $20 buys a higher ceiling on the same agent.

## ChatGPT: One Allowance for Chat, Work, and Code

OpenAI retired the old ChatGPT agent mode in 2026 and replaced it with ChatGPT Work, which shares one allowance with Codex, OpenAI’s coding agent. On Plus, that allowance is metered on a five-hour window plus a weekly cap, and OpenAI publishes ranges rather than counts, because a short question and a long agent run cost very different amounts. Deep research is “limited” on Free and Go and “expanded” on Plus, in OpenAI’s own wording, with no published number of reports.

The published numbers live in scheduled tasks: 3 active tasks on Free and Go, 5 on Plus, and 15 on Pro. Free tasks run at most once a day in a flexible time window, while exact times and hourly runs need a paid plan. Go, at $8 a month, is a budget rung: more messages, uploads, images, and voice than Free, though the plan may include ads.

## Claude: Scheduling Is a Paid Feature

Claude’s free tier is a chatbot with tools: web search, file creation, code execution, memory, connectors, and up to five Projects, all inside a limited five-hour and weekly allowance. The agent-flavored features sit behind Pro. Anthropic’s pricing page lists “hand off and schedule tasks” as a Pro feature, and Claude in Chrome, which lets Claude act in your browser, is also paid. Pro costs $20 a month billed monthly, or $17 a month on annual billing, and Anthropic’s FAQ says Pro gives at least five times more usage per five-hour session than Free. Max at $100 a month multiplies Pro’s usage by five, and Ultra at $200 multiplies it by ten per week and drops the five-hour limit for verified accounts.

Claude’s free tier is generous for light use. If you want work done on a schedule, Claude has no free rung at all.

## Gemini: The Cheapest Way to Buy More Usage

Google sells the lowest entry price in this comparison. AI Plus costs $4.99 a month and doubles the free tier’s limits. AI Pro at $19.99 quadruples them, and Ultra runs from $99.99 a month at five times Pro’s limits to $199.99 at twenty times Pro’s limits. The free tier includes Deep Research, the report-writing feature that ChatGPT limits and Claude charges for, although free Deep Research can be unavailable at peak demand, and scheduled actions sit on the paid plans.

Google meters the same way, with a five-hour refresh up to a weekly cap, and every paid tier is a multiple of a free tier whose exact size is not published either.

## Copilot: Agents Start at $19.99, Inside a Microsoft 365 Bundle

Microsoft no longer sells a standalone Copilot subscription. Copilot comes inside Microsoft 365 plans, and the agents, Researcher and Analyst, are not available on the $9.99 Personal or $12.99 Family plans at all. They start on Premium at $19.99 a month, where Microsoft’s support table lists them as “Extensive usage,” a label Microsoft does not convert into a number. If your life already runs on Word, Excel, and Outlook, Premium is a reasonable way to buy agent features. It is also the least transparent pricing in this comparison for a standalone agent purchase.

## The Verdict: Buy the Ceiling That Matches Your Habits

There is no honest tasks-per-dollar ranking, because four of these six companies refuse to publish the unit you would rank by. A ranking by what your money actually changes:

- **You want an agent working for you daily, on a schedule, connected to your email and calendar:** Muse. The free tier includes the whole feature set, and Power’s $20 buys a bigger weekly allowance for the same agent. No other plan in this comparison works that way.
- **You want research reports:** Gemini. Deep Research is in the free tier, AI Pro at $19.99 raises every limit fourfold, and eligible US college students can get [a year of AI Pro free](/articles/best-ai-agent-for-college-students/), which makes it the best research deal running.
- **You want the cheapest paid upgrade:** AI Plus at $4.99 or ChatGPT Go at $8, depending on which free tier you already live in.
- **You write and code with the agent all day:** Claude Pro on annual billing at $17 a month is the cheapest $20-class plan, and its five-hour window suits long working sessions. ChatGPT Plus is the pick if you split time between chat, agent work, and Codex, since one allowance covers all three.
- **You live in Microsoft 365:** Premium at $19.99, and accept that Microsoft will not tell you exactly how much agent usage you bought.

Whichever plan you pick, watch the first month. Every one of these companies will tell you when you hit a limit; the useful information is how often that happens at your real workload, and that number is yours, not theirs.
